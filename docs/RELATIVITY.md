# Optional extension: special relativity

An electron-following viewpoint can explain the force around an idealized steady
wire that is neutral in the laboratory frame.
Einstein's theory here is **special relativity**; neither observer is absolute.

| Laboratory viewpoint | Viewpoint moving with the mean electron drift |
| --- | --- |
| Positive ion lattice is at rest. | Positive lattice moves backwards; longitudinal spacing is contracted. |
| Electrons have a net drift. | Mean electron drift is zero; longitudinal electron spacing is larger than in the laboratory description. |
| Idealized wire is neutral. | Transformed charge densities give a net positive charge per unit length. |

These are different simultaneous position measurements of the same current-
carrying system, not a physical squeeze caused by closing the switch. Random
microscopic electron motion is omitted in this simple thought experiment.

An external electron travelling alongside the flow is attracted magnetically
in the laboratory description. In its rest frame, electric attraction explains
its initial force. A magnetic field can still exist there; zero velocity makes
the magnetic force on that test charge zero. Ordinary drift makes the spacing
effect far too small to draw to scale.

Sources: [MIT, Magnetism and Relativity](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-a08-attraction-and-repulsion-the-magic-of-magnets-fall-2005/8b381002f65584aef2ba0b42339868aa_mag_relativity.pdf),
[Feynman Lectures II, section 13–6](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_13.html#Ch13-S6).

## Suggested follow-up for Class 8 (not included in the main animation)

1. Establish the neutral wire and electron drift in the familiar classroom view.
2. Introduce a separate probe electron outside the wire, travelling alongside it.
3. Pause and announce: **Now travel alongside the electron flow.**
4. Change the frame label before illustrating different measured spacings.
5. Use a clearly marked, exaggerated effect and an electric-force arrow on the probe.
6. Compare the two descriptions of the same interaction, then return to the lab view.

A camera zoom alone changes magnification, not the inertial reference frame.
The main lesson therefore keeps its ion spacing fixed and labels itself LAB VIEW.
